# Other Productivity Applications

**Freedom** — Cross-device blocker (desktop + mobile) with scheduling and a
"locked mode" that stops you from turning it off mid-session. It's one of the
most recognized app and website blockers, known for cross-device blocking and
locked mode, but it's also one of the pricier tools in the category at around
$120/year. It's purely a blocker — no routines, no habit-building layer.

**Cold Turkey** — The "nuclear option" for blocking. You create block lists of
websites and apps, set a schedule, and there's even a "Frozen Turkey" mode that
locks you out of your entire computer until the block period ends. It's a
one-time purchase rather than a subscription, and stores data locally. Extremely
strict, but desktop-only and has no habit/routine features at all.

**Tiimo** — A visual, neurodivergent-first daily planner. It centers flexibility
and accessibility, with mood tracking and daily reviews to help people stay
present and reflect. Its colour-coded visual timeline and per-activity countdown
timers address ADHD "time blindness" by making time visible rather than
abstract. It leans heavily on an AI co-planner for task breakdown as a headline
feature, and at roughly $54–80/year it sits above the mid-tier, and going beyond
the daily task view often means reaching for a second app anyway.

**Routinery** — A step-by-step routine executor, not a planner. You build a
routine as an ordered list of timed steps, and the app walks you through each
one with a countdown timer and visual progress bar. The most common complaint is
an increasingly aggressive paywall, with features quietly moved from free to
premium over time.

**Focus Bear** — Combines distraction blocking and guided routines in one app.
It builds your routines, blocks distractions across all your devices, and nudges
you back on track, and a block started on one device applies across the rest
automatically. Its feature set spans cross-device blocking, real-time habit
guidance for morning/evening routines, a "Grizzly Bear" strict mode, and
Pomodoro mode. Notably, it explicitly does not log keystrokes or capture your
screen, and break reminders are call-aware so they stay quiet during meetings.

# What makes Focus Bear different

It's the only one of these that seriously tries to do both jobs, hard blocking
(like Freedom/Cold Turkey) and structured routine-building (like
Routinery/Tiimo) in a single app, built specifically around ADHD/autistic
executive-function patterns rather than general "productivity."

# Why choose Focus Bear over the others

If your problem is that you have both a distraction problem and a "can't
start/stick to my routine" problem, buying Freedom + Routinery separately means
two subscriptions, two logins, and no shared data between them. Focus Bear is
the one app that covers both without stitching tools together, and it's cheaper
than paying for two specialist apps.

# Where a competitor wins

Cold Turkey wins on raw blocking strength. Its "Frozen Turkey" mode and
one-time-payment model are genuinely more extreme and harder to bypass than
anything Focus Bear offers, if pure willpower-proof blocking is all you want,
Cold Turkey is the sharper tool, and cheaper long-term since it's not a
subscription.

# Improvement Focus Bear could make

While Focus Bear already has scaled friction through its Cuddly Bear/Grizzly
Bear modes and a "break-glass" emergency override, the friction in Grizzly Bear
mode is currently a passive wait timer, which doesn't really interrupt the
impulsive, autopilot mental state that leads to compulsive checking, you can
just sit and wait it out. Replacing or supplementing that with an active-task
friction, like a simple captcha or a short typed confirmation, would force a
brief moment of conscious engagement instead, which is more effective at
breaking the automatic reach-for-the-phone habit without being punishing or
overly restrictive, which matters given the app's ADHD/autistic user base.
