# Best Practices for Troubleshooting Coding Problems

When troubleshooting a coding problem, I think it is important to first
understand the problem before immediately asking for help. I would start by
reproducing the issue, reading the error message carefully, checking my own
code, and forming a hypothesis about what might be causing the problem. I would
then try a solution myself and use documentation or Google to research the
problem. If I am still stuck, I would consider asking a colleague or using an AI
tool depending on the situation. After finding a solution, I would try to
understand why it worked rather than simply applying it.

# My Perspective on Using AI in Coding

I believe AI can be helpful in coding, but I am somewhat skeptical of relying on
it too heavily. I see AI as an extension of my own thinking rather than a
replacement for it. AI can be useful when I need another perspective,
particularly when another developer is not available to discuss a problem with.
However, I think relying on AI too often can reduce independent problem-solving
and critical-thinking skills. There is a difference between using AI to
challenge my reasoning and asking it to do the reasoning for me. I therefore
prefer to use AI after I have already spent some time understanding and
investigating the problem myself.

# Complexity, Sensitivity and Urgency

The complexity of the problem affects which resource I use. For a simple error
or unfamiliar concept, documentation or Google may be enough. For a complex
problem where I need to discuss different approaches, AI can provide another
perspective, while a colleague may be more appropriate when project-specific
knowledge is required.

The sensitivity of the information is also important. I should not put
passwords, API keys, private user information, proprietary code, or other
confidential information into an AI tool or public search. In these situations,
I would use approved internal documentation or ask an appropriate colleague.

Urgency also affects my decision. If the problem is not urgent, I prefer to
spend more time researching and understanding it myself. If the problem is
urgent and I have already attempted to troubleshoot it, I would seek help sooner
rather than spending too much time stuck on the same issue.

# Reflection

## When Do I Prefer Using AI vs. Searching Google?

I prefer using Google and official documentation when I need factual
information, documentation, examples, or a solution that can be researched from
existing sources. Searching also allows me to compare different sources and
understand how other developers have approached a problem.

I prefer using AI when I need an interactive discussion about a specific
problem. For example, I can explain my approach and ask AI to identify
weaknesses in my process. This can be particularly useful when I do not have
another developer available to discuss the problem with. However, I would still
verify the information provided by AI rather than assuming that it is correct.

## How Do I Decide When to Ask a Colleague Instead?

I would ask a colleague when I have already investigated the problem but need
another developer's experience or perspective. I would also prefer to ask a
colleague when the problem involves an important architectural decision,
project-specific knowledge, sensitive information, or something that could have
significant consequences if implemented incorrectly. A colleague may understand
the project's context and requirements better than a general-purpose AI tool.

## Challenges of Troubleshooting Alone

Troubleshooting alone can be difficult because it is easy to become stuck in one
way of thinking. Without another person to challenge my assumptions, I may
continue investigating the wrong part of the system or overlook a simpler
explanation. It can also take longer to find the relevant information when I do
not know what to search for. AI can help provide another perspective when a
colleague is unavailable, but I think it is important not to become dependent on
it. The goal should be to use available resources to solve problems while
continuing to develop my own troubleshooting and problem-solving skills.
