# Working with Vulnerable Populations

## Research & Learn
### Vulnerable Populations
Vulnerable populations can include people with disabilities, neurodivergent individuals, children, older people and people experiencing mental health or social difficulties. They may face accessibility, communication, privacy or information overload challenges online.

### Ethical Considerations
When working with neurodivergent people, I should avoid making assumptions and respect individual needs. Designs should reduce cognitive overload, avoid unnecessary distractions and provide clear and predictable interactions.

### Accessibility
For people with ADHD or Autism, I can use simple language, clear instructions, predictable navigation, readable layouts and fewer unnecessary distractions.

### Supporting Neurodivergent Teammates
I should communicate clearly, respect different working styles, avoid unnecessary pressure and allow reasonable flexibility when possible.

## Reflection
I can make my communication more inclusive by using clear language, breaking information into smaller steps and asking if somone needs clarification.

Common accessibility issues to consider in Focus Bear include too many notifications, unclear instructions, unnecessary distractions, complicated navigation and too much information on one screen. These could increase cognitive load for users with ADHD or Autism.

One practical change I can make is to keep interfaces and instructions simple, precise and focused, so users can understand what they need to do without unnecessary cognitive load.

## Task
### First-Person Account
A common theme in first-person accounts from people with ADHD is that productivity tools can become overwhelming when they contain too many tasks, reminders or complicated features. Simple systems are often easier to maintain.

### Design Improvement
I would improve Focus Bear by making important actions clear and reducing unnecessary information or distractions on each screen.

### Supportive User Response
It’s okay if you’re struggling to get started. Try focusing on just one small task first. You don't need to complete everything at once. Take your time, and celebrate small progress.
